# VizSkillLab v0.1 — synthesis report

A literature-grounded, renderer-independent visual-reasoning skill stack. This report
follows the project brief §24. It summarizes what the source corpus taught, why the
skills are drawn where they are, what is treated as non-negotiable, and what the next
research pass should attack.

Scope of v0.1: 17 source cards → a 90-principle machine-readable registry → 6 skills →
an evaluation suite (7-level failure taxonomy, 12 hard gates + 100-pt rubric, 6
behavioral test cases). Renderer-independent throughout; no toolkit is named in any
rule.

---

## A. What we learned — the ideas that shaped the design

1. **Failure is layered, and a fix at the wrong layer is not a fix** (Munzner's nested
   model, S01). Restyling cannot cure a wrong-abstraction error. This became the whole
   architecture: a 7-level taxonomy and skills that own specific levels.
2. **Validity and quality are different questions.** Some defects make a figure *lie or
   fail to be read* (truncated bars, undisclosed simulation, color-only cues); others
   merely make it *worse*. This forced the hard-gate / soft-rubric split (Draco, S10)
   instead of one weighted score.
3. **Encoding accuracy has a channel ordering** — position and length are read far more
   accurately than area, angle, or intensity (S02, S15). This is the single most
   reused idea (ENC-01) and the reason pie/area encodings are demoted.
4. **Bars and lines are not the same honesty problem.** A zero baseline is a hard law
   for bars and a judgment call for lines — the split (ENC-02 vs UNC-01) came directly
   from taking the sources literally rather than over-generalizing.
5. **Rainbow colormaps actively mislead**, they are not merely dated (S05). They
   fabricate gradients and die in grayscale/CVD — hence a hard gate, not a preference.
6. **Perceptual uniformity and monotonic lightness** are what make a continuous colormap
   honest; "survives grayscale" is a mechanical, checkable proxy (S05, ACCESS-05).
7. **Accessibility is a correctness constraint, not a courtesy.** "Color never the sole
   cue" is hard because ~8% of male readers and every grayscale printout depend on it.
8. **Direct labeling beats detached legends** (S04, S15) — it removes a lookup the
   reader would otherwise perform, and it degrades gracefully.
9. **The medium changes the right answer.** A correct print figure can be an unreadable
   projected slide (viewing distance, type size, series count). Medium is an explicit
   input, not an afterthought (MEDIA-*).
10. **Uncertainty and simulation must be visible in the mark, not just the caption**
    (SCI/UNC families) — a projection drawn identically to data is a scientific lie even
    if the caption discloses it.
11. **Network data is often not a node-link graph.** Dense relationships belong in a
    matrix; a hairball asserts structure that isn't there (NET-02/03).
12. **The render is the ground truth.** Intent can be perfect and the exported artifact
    still broken (clipped legend, colliding labels, shifted colors). "Inspect the actual
    render" (QA-08) is a hard gate, and it is what makes the stack renderer-independent.
13. **Read-back is the only guard for the invisible failures.** Levels 1, 2, and 7 don't
    show up in the pixels; asking "what takeaway does a naive reader leave with?" (QA-05)
    is the safeguard.
14. **A rule you can't check is a preference.** Every principle carries an executable
    `check`; principles that couldn't be made checkable were softened or flagged.

## B. Skill architecture — why the boundaries fall where they do

Six skills, each owning a contiguous band of the reasoning sequence and a level of the
failure taxonomy, so a defect routes to exactly one owner:

- **visual-intent** — problem, audience, medium, data abstraction, reader task. Owns
  Levels 1–2 (the expensive, invisible failures). Everything downstream depends on its
  output, so it runs first and its intermediate representation is the contract.
- **visual-encoding** — chart family, channel assignment, color, accessibility. Owns the
  bulk of Level 3. This is where the channel-ordering and colormap laws live.
- **visual-composition** — hierarchy, layout, figure-ground, **and annotation** (folded
  in for v0.1, D4). Owns Level 4. Annotation sits here because label-vs-legend is a
  layout decision as much as a text one.
- **scientific-visualization** — colormaps-as-science, uncertainty, simulation
  disclosure, networks, diagrams, maps. Owns Level 5, the domain-integrity failures.
- **visual-render-qa** — renderer-independent inspection of the actual output. Owns
  Level 6 and enforces all 12 hard gates. This is the validity boundary.
- **visual-critic** — orchestrator. Holds the case file, applies the taxonomy + rubric,
  owns the SHIP/REPAIR/ESCALATE verdict and Level 7. It invokes the others rather than
  restating their rules (D5), keeping the registry the single source of truth.

The line between `visual-encoding` and `scientific-visualization` is drawn at
*domain knowledge*: generic channel/color choices are encoding; anything that needs to
know it's a temperature field, a projection, or a pipeline diagram is scientific.

## C. Hard constraints — what is non-negotiable, and why

Thirteen gates (RUBRIC.md G1–G13). Each blocks SHIP regardless of rubric score because
each marks a figure that lies, can't be read, or excludes readers:

- **G1 magnitude honesty** — no truncated bars, radius-scaled areas, 3-D quantity, or
  hidden breaks (ENC-02, SCI-02).
- **G2 quantifiability** — a quantitative scale needs its color bar / units / ticks.
- **G3 color-accessibility** — color is never the sole cue; must survive grayscale/CVD.
- **G4 text contrast** — measured contrast meets the adopted medium floor.
- **G5 colormap validity** — no rainbow/jet or non-uniform map on a data dimension.
- **G6 scientific disclosure** — simulation/exaggeration/non-scale sizing is disclosed
  *and* visually distinct.
- **G7 hierarchy exists** — there is one identifiable primary element.
- **G8 render integrity** — no collisions, clipping, or occlusion in the actual output.
- **G9 diagram/network legibility** — declared vocabulary; matrix over hairball where
  density demands it.
- **G10 self-containment** — a published figure carries a stand-alone caption/source.
- **G11 intent match** — the read-back takeaway equals the intended takeaway.
- **G12 claim–encoding consistency** — every headline/annotation number is in the same
  reference frame (baseline, units, scope) the marks encode, or that frame is drawn on
  the figure. Added in v0.1.1 after the ablation exposed a title asserting "warming since
  the late 1800s" over an axis of anomalies vs a 1951–1980 baseline (SCI-06).
- **G13 aggregation honesty** — a pooled summary/fit is never passed off as a per-subgroup
  description, and a decomposition variable that doesn't vary is never used to explain a
  difference (Simpson's paradox / ecological fallacy); show the disaggregated view or
  disclose the summary as pooled. Added after the 2026-09-01 bake-off flagged pooled-fit-as-
  group-description as its highest-value catch (SCI-07).

One item is deliberately **ESCALATE, not a gate**: the non-bar zero-baseline decision
(UNC-01). It is a genuine ratio-vs-difference judgment that belongs to the user.

## D. Strong heuristics — normally applies, context can override

Soft (SHOULD/MAY) principles cost rubric points but never block SHIP: the channel
ordering beyond the hard cases (ENC-01), direct labeling over legends (ANNO-01),
minimizing non-data ink balanced against reader-serving scaffolding (PERC-01 vs the
minimalism/scaffolding tension), small multiples over overplotting, restrained
categorical palettes, subtle reference lines, and medium-tuning beyond the legibility
floor. These are where taste and context legitimately vary, and where the rubric — not
a gate — does the work.

## E. Known conflicts — where good sources disagree

Recorded in full in `conflicts.md` (9 conflicts). The load-bearing ones:

- **Minimalism vs. scaffolding** — Tufte-style data-ink reduction vs. the reader aids
  (gridlines, reference lines, annotation) that genuinely help. Resolved by
  "reader-serving purpose," which is judgment-heavy and flagged for a sharper test.
- **Zero baseline** — universal for bars, contested for everything else → the ENC-02 /
  UNC-01 split (D1).
- **Rainbow "never" vs. "avoid unless justified"** — we took the stronger position on
  data dimensions (D2).
- **WCAG web norms vs. figures/slides** — kept the floor as a principle, left the number
  medium-dependent (D3).
- **Standard vs. bespoke notation**, **patterns vs. solid fills**, **3-D avoidance vs.
  NASA SVS cinematic renders** — each resolved by context (audience, medium, whether the
  third dimension carries data).

## F. Weak spots — where evidence or operational guidance is thin

- **Exact channel ordering for area vs. angle** — the robust part is encoded; the fine
  rank rests on Cleveland & McGill 1984, which is paywalled (reference_only). ENC-01
  flags this rather than inventing the order.
- **Rubric weights are the brief's defaults, not learned** — QA-04 wants
  perceptually-grounded weights (Draco's learned weights are the intended source, not
  yet imported). Until then the 100-pt split is reasoned, not measured.
- **Per-medium contrast/size floors** are unspecified numbers (D3) — G4 is currently
  judgment-assisted rather than fully mechanical.
- **Minimalism-vs-scaffolding** and **self-containment** lean on judgment; both need a
  crisper operational test to reduce inter-rater variance.
- **Automated collision/contrast scanning** vs. a vision check of the render is
  unresolved tooling — v0.1 assumes a rendered-image inspection step exists.
- **Canonical books** (Tufte, Few, Munzner textbook, S14 Jambor) were not lawfully
  acquirable in-sandbox; their ideas are cross-checked secondhand against accessible
  Tier-A/B sources, so some nuance is indirect.

## G. Benchmark readiness — what can be run now and what a pass means

Runnable today: the **6 behavioral test cases** (TEST_CASES.md), each pinned to specific
gates — truncated bar (G1), rainbow SST heatmap (G5+G3), 9-slice pie (G3), undocumented
pipeline diagram (G9), simulation-shown-as-data (G6+G11), unreadable projected slide
(G8+G4). Driven manually through `visual-critic`.

A meaningful **pass** = the expected gate(s) fire *and* the final SHIP artifact meets the
stated correct outcome *and* the read-back takeaway matches intent. This validates that
the gates and the routing work — it does not yet measure aesthetic quality or compare
against a baseline. Gates G2, G7, G10 are exercised incidentally (every case's figure
must still clear them) and get dedicated cases in v0.2.

What v0.1 explicitly does **not** yet prove: that the stack beats a naive baseline
(needs the ablation), that the rubric is reproducible across raters (needs inter-rater
scoring), and that it agrees with validated designer work (needs the
validation-set pass over exemplar decks — held out, never an input).

## H. Recommended v0.2 work — highest-leverage next steps

1. **Import Draco's learned soft-cost weights (S10)** to replace the brief-default rubric
   weights — turns the 100-pt layer from reasoned to evidence-grounded (closes QA-04).
2. **Build the ablation + inter-rater + regression harness** (BENCHMARK_PLAN §v0.2) and
   expand to the 12-case suite with dedicated G2/G7/G10 and Level-1/2/7 cases. This is
   what converts "the gates fire" into "the stack demonstrably helps."
3. **Add a per-medium contrast/size floor table** so G4 becomes mechanical instead of
   judgment-assisted.
4. **Lawfully obtain the reference_only sources** (Cleveland & McGill, S14) to pin the
   channel ordering and sharpen ANNO/QA checks — only if obtainable, never fabricated.
5. **Run the exemplar validation-set pass**: score a sample of the accepted
   decks; investigate any slide the stack would REPAIR as a signal to adjust a
   principle's force or add a context exception (not to overrule the exemplar).
6. **Decide packaging** (one umbrella skill vs. six installable skills) — deferred to
   this review point by project decision; see the recommendation in the delivery note.
7. **Operationalize the two soft-but-fuzzy rules** (minimalism-vs-scaffolding,
   self-containment) with sharper tests to cut inter-rater variance.
8. **Resolve the render-inspection tooling question** — decide how much collision/
   contrast checking is automated vs. a vision check, and emit a machine-readable
   defect list from critique mode.
